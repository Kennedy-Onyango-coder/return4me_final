# Security and privacy incident response checklist

1. Preserve safety first. If there is immediate risk to a person, follow the organisation's emergency and safeguarding procedure.
2. Record the incident in the restricted incident register: discovery time, reporter, affected service/data classes, observed facts and immediate containment. Do not include passwords, tokens, full M-Pesa numbers or unnecessary identity data.
3. Assign an incident lead and restrict access to the investigation record. Do not treat automated risk signals as proof of wrongdoing.
4. Contain the issue: revoke affected sessions/credentials, disable affected upload/payment/publishing feature flags, restrict affected routes or storage access, and preserve immutable audit/custody/ledger evidence.
5. Establish scope and impact from evidence. Preserve logs and timestamps; do not edit or delete evidence to make the incident disappear.
6. Escalate to the privacy/legal lead and qualified Kenyan counsel to assess controller/processor duties, ODPC notification, data-subject notification and statutory timing. Do not invent a notification deadline.
7. For financial incidents, contact the provider using verified official channels, freeze affected payout legs where possible, reconcile exact provider references and do not resubmit ambiguous transactions.
8. Document containment, decisions, notifications, remediation, owner, due dates and retest evidence. Run a lessons-learned review and update controls/runbooks.

This is a response checklist, not a legally approved breach-notification policy. An incident exercise and counsel review remain required.
